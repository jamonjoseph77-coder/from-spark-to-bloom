# Cinema Business Model
## Working strategy document — November 2026

## 1. Executive summary

**Cinema — Experience The World Of Stories** is a digital entertainment home where people discover and watch a broad range of authorised stories in one place: movies, series, documentaries, factual programmes, news, sports, live television, shorts, creator programmes and more.

Cinema's intended consumer proposition is simple: viewers can access the experience without a recurring viewer subscription or pay-per-title checkout. The business earns from commercial partnerships around the content and audience experience, rather than charging viewers to enter.

Cinema is not positioned as a conventional subscription streaming service or a short-video social network. It brings a broad television and storytelling experience into one digital destination, across mobile, web and connected screens.

This is a working model, not a claim that Cinema already has a licensed catalogue, active advertiser demand, revenue, or completed commercial agreements.

## 2. The problem Cinema addresses

Viewers encounter fragmented entertainment: different services for films, series, documentaries, live channels, sports, news and creator programming. They may also face subscription barriers, scattered discovery and difficulty finding stories from different markets.

Content owners, independent producers and publishers can struggle to reach new audiences, distribute catalogues across markets and package their work for commercial partnerships.

Brands need relevant, brand-safe environments in which to support entertainment and storytelling without relying only on disruptive interruptions or generic banner placements.

Cinema aims to connect these needs through one accessible entertainment destination, an authorised content catalogue, structured discovery and a distinctive advertising and partnership system.

## 3. Customers and stakeholders

### Viewers
People who want to discover and watch stories across genres, formats and markets without a viewer subscription.

### Brands and agencies
Companies seeking relevant, clearly disclosed sponsorship opportunities around titles, collections, premieres, channels and selected Cinema placements.

### Studios, producers and distributors
Rights holders who want authorised distribution, audience reach and potential commercial participation for eligible titles.

### Creators, publishers and channel operators
People and organisations who want to build an audience through a Cinema channel or submit eligible programmes and short-form content under Cinema's publishing rules.

### Advertisers and technology partners
Programmatic advertising providers and other commercial infrastructure partners that may help deliver eligible advertising demand and reporting.

## 4. Value propositions

| Stakeholder | Cinema's proposed value |
|---|---|
| Viewers | One accessible home for a broad range of stories and entertainment |
| Brands | Distinctive, contextual sponsorship products with clear reporting |
| Studios and rights holders | Additional authorised distribution and potential commercial participation |
| Creators and publishers | A channel and discovery environment for eligible content |
| Agencies | A new set of story-centred sponsorship products to plan and buy |

The value must be demonstrated through working playback, audience usage, content quality, rights compliance and measurable campaign delivery.

## 5. Revenue model

Cinema's primary planned revenue engine is B2B advertising and brand partnerships. Viewer access is intended to remain free.

### A. Direct brand partnerships — primary differentiated revenue
Cinema sells clearly defined sponsorship products directly to brands and agencies:
- **Title Sponsorship:** a brand sponsors the presentation or title hub of a specific licensed title.
- **Contextual Brand Partnership:** a brand supports a story, topic or collection relevant to its category.
- **Sponsored Collections:** a brand supports a curated collection while Cinema retains editorial control.
- **Premiere Partnerships:** a brand sponsors an authorised premiere or special screening window.
- **Channel Partnerships:** a brand supports an eligible creator, studio or publisher channel.
- **Home and Category Partnerships:** limited, clearly labelled placements in selected Cinema destinations.
- **Special Story Campaigns:** custom campaigns around factual programming, cultural events or other approved editorial packages.

Commercial structures may include a fixed package fee, a negotiated CPM for measurable inventory, or a hybrid sponsorship package. Prices should be based on actual audience and delivery data, not invented reach claims.

### B. Programmatic advertising — supporting revenue
Cinema may use an eligible publisher advertising network for standard placements that fit the product and its policies.

Google AdSense may be assessed as a simpler starting point for eligible standard web inventory. Google Ad Manager may become useful when Cinema has direct campaigns, multiple demand sources and the operational capacity to manage them.

Google is a potential demand and ad-serving partner, not the owner of Cinema's advertising products. It does not automatically supply Title Sponsorships, guarantee a brand for a particular film, or guarantee revenue.

Programmatic placements are optional supporting inventory. They must not turn Cinema into a banner-heavy site or introduce unwanted interruptions.

### C. Distribution and content partnerships — conditional
Cinema may negotiate distribution, licensing or commercial arrangements with studios, distributors, broadcasters and publishers. These are not automatic revenue streams. Any commission, platform fee, minimum guarantee or revenue share must be agreed in writing with the relevant rights holder and must be permitted by the underlying rights.

### D. Future B2B services — later opportunity
Once the platform has proven audience and operational capability, Cinema could consider paid campaign services such as campaign production, sponsored collection design, audience reporting or distribution support. These services should only be offered when Cinema can reliably deliver them.

Cinema is not currently relying on viewer subscriptions, pay-per-title checkout, tokenisation or an investment marketplace.

## 6. How the commercial engine works

1. Cinema secures and verifies the rights needed to make a title available in the intended territory and period.
2. Cinema classifies the title by format, category, language, territory, audience suitability and permitted commercial uses.
3. Cinema packages eligible placements and sponsorship products.
4. A brand or agency submits an enquiry or buys an eligible campaign through an agreed process.
5. Cinema checks the advertiser, category, creative, placement, title rights, dates and territory.
6. The campaign is approved and scheduled.
7. Cinema delivers the agreed placements without interrupting playback.
8. Cinema measures agreed campaign events and reports delivery.
9. Cinema invoices and collects payment through an authorised company process.
10. Cinema pays any agreed rights-holder or supplier obligations and measures net contribution.

For programmatic advertising, the connected provider handles the eligible auction and ad-serving process; Cinema still controls its own placement design, privacy choices and integration boundaries.

## 7. Content supply and rights strategy

Cinema needs real, authorised and playable content. Posters, metadata, trailers and discovery listings do not constitute a playable catalogue.

Potential supply routes:
- Direct non-exclusive licensing from studios and independent producers.
- Distribution agreements with catalogue owners.
- Broadcaster and publisher partnerships for eligible programmes.
- Creator and channel submissions under clear publishing terms.
- Openly licensed or public-domain works only after exact-edition and component-rights checks.

Each title requires a rights record that identifies the exact work and edition, territories, term, streaming/rehosting permission, playback method, languages/subtitles, artwork and promotional rights, advertising/sponsorship permissions, exclusivity, reporting and takedown obligations.

A streaming licence does not automatically grant the right to sell title sponsorship or use a sponsor's branding alongside a title. Those permissions must be checked separately.

Cinema should begin with a focused, rights-cleared pilot catalogue and expand as content supply, playback reliability and audience demand are proven. Do not publish titles merely because a URL or public listing exists.

## 8. Brand and marketplace strategy

The planned **Cinema Presence Marketplace** is the business-facing destination where brands and agencies can understand Cinema's audience proposition and explore sponsorship products.

Initial public-facing sections:
- Cinema's positioning and story-led entertainment proposition.
- Advertising products: Title Sponsorship, Contextual Partnerships, Sponsored Collections, Premiere Partnerships, Channel Partnerships and selected Home placements.
- How campaigns work, including approval, disclosure and reporting.
- Example placement mock-ups clearly marked as examples, not live campaigns.
- Brand/agency enquiry form with business contact, campaign objective, category, territory, budget range (optional), dates and message.
- Contact and partnership information.

Later capabilities, only when operationally justified:
- Advertiser accounts and saved campaign briefs.
- A catalogue of available sponsorship opportunities.
- Campaign proposals and approvals.
- Creative asset upload and review.
- Delivery reporting and invoice status.

The marketplace must not imply guaranteed audience numbers, available titles, advertiser access or campaign results that Cinema cannot substantiate. It should initially generate qualified enquiries, not pretend to be a fully automated ad exchange.

## 9. Go-to-market sequence

### Phase 1 — readiness
- Confirm the product works reliably on mobile and web.
- Confirm a small set of authorised, playable titles.
- Complete title-level rights and commercial-use records.
- Finalise the first three sponsorship products.
- Prepare example mock-ups, a concise brand deck and a partnership enquiry route.

### Phase 2 — pilot supply and demand
- Secure a small number of rights-cleared titles with terms that permit the intended distribution and advertising activity.
- Approach a focused group of relevant brands and agencies.
- Offer a limited pilot with defined placements, dates, reporting and deliverables.
- Avoid promising reach or revenue before audience data exists.
- Measure campaign performance and viewer experience.

### Phase 3 — repeatable operations
- Build the Cinema campaign-management workspace.
- Standardise rights checks, advertiser approval, campaign approvals, invoicing and reporting.
- Evaluate AdSense for suitable standard inventory.
- Evaluate Ad Manager when inventory and direct sales justify the complexity.
- Expand content categories and markets based on proven supply and demand.

### Phase 4 — scale
- Expand the catalogue and distribution partnerships.
- Develop repeatable brand packages and seasonal campaigns.
- Add advertiser self-service capabilities only after Cinema has a reliable campaign operation.
- Expand into additional territories only after rights, compliance, localisation and delivery are ready.

## 10. Unit economics and financial discipline

Cinema should track each title and campaign separately.

### Campaign contribution
`Campaign contribution = cash collected − rights-holder/supplier obligations − ad-serving/payment costs − campaign production/delivery costs − attributable operating costs`

### Programmatic estimate
`Estimated publisher revenue = eligible impressions / 1,000 × realised publisher eCPM`

These are planning formulas, not revenue forecasts. eCPM, fill rate, campaign sell-through, payment timing and costs must come from real operating data.

For every title, track:
- Rights acquisition and renewal cost.
- Delivery, storage, encoding and bandwidth cost.
- Permitted territories and licence term.
- Whether sponsorship and advertising are permitted.
- Viewing starts, completion and repeat usage.
- Campaign revenue attributable to the title, if any.
- Net contribution after agreed obligations and operating costs.

Do not accept a rights deal based on headline revenue share alone. Model the full delivery cost, rights term, commercial permissions and expected audience. Do not assume a standard percentage for the rights holder.

## 11. Key metrics

### Viewer and product
- Monthly active viewers and returning viewers.
- Playback starts, successful starts and completion.
- Search-to-play conversion and discovery engagement.
- Playback errors, startup time and buffering.
- Usage by device, category and territory.

### Content supply
- Number of rights-cleared playable titles.
- Share of catalogue with complete rights metadata.
- Time to onboard a title.
- Rights renewal and expiry pipeline.
- Cost per playable hour and per completed view.

### Commercial
- Qualified brand enquiries.
- Proposals issued and campaign win rate.
- Direct campaign revenue contracted and collected.
- Campaign delivery and renewal rate.
- Programmatic fill rate, viewability and realised eCPM.
- Net contribution by campaign and title.
- Receivables and payment delays.

### Trust and compliance
- Rights exceptions and takedown response time.
- Ad complaints and accidental clicks.
- Sponsor conflicts and editorial complaints.
- Privacy incidents and policy violations.

## 12. Main risks and responses

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Not enough authorised content | Start with a small rights-cleared pilot; do not confuse discovery listings with playable rights |
| Low audience means weak ad revenue | Do not forecast meaningful programmatic revenue before usage exists; focus on direct pilot partnerships with honest deliverables |
| Rights do not permit sponsorship | Add explicit advertising and brand-association checks to every title record |
| Google approval or demand is unavailable | Keep direct sponsorship as an independent path; do not make Google a launch dependency |
| Playback and delivery costs exceed revenue | Measure per-title delivery costs and pilot economics before scaling |
| Brand conflicts damage trust | Use sponsor-category rules, clear labels and editorial independence |
| Marketplace overpromises inventory | Show examples as examples; list only confirmed opportunities and substantiated metrics |
| Privacy and youth-safety risk | Use contextual advertising, data minimisation, age-appropriate controls and adult/legal review |

## 13. What Cinema should build first

1. A reliable viewer experience and a real rights-cleared playable catalogue.
2. A title rights and commercial-permissions record.
3. The first three sponsorship products: Title Sponsorship, Contextual Brand Partnership and Sponsored Collections.
4. Three to five inactive-by-default advertising placements with clear disclosure.
5. A simple Cinema Presence Marketplace website that explains products and captures enquiries.
6. A lightweight internal campaign approval and reporting process.
7. Only then, a suitable programmatic advertising integration.

## 14. Current assumptions and open decisions

- Viewer access is intended to remain free; no viewer subscription or pay-per-title checkout is planned.
- Direct brand partnerships are the primary differentiated commercial proposition.
- Programmatic advertising is supplementary and conditional on eligibility and actual demand.
- Cinema will not use intrusive mid-rolls as its default advertising model.
- Content availability and commercial sponsorship permissions must be verified title by title.
- Initial territories, audience targets, rate cards, rights-holder economics and launch dates are not yet fixed.
- All contracts, publisher accounts, financial administration, tax and payment commitments must be handled by an authorised adult/company representative and reviewed appropriately.

## 15. Strategic principle

**Cinema should monetise the value it creates around stories — not interrupt the stories themselves.**

The long-term advantage is the combination of a broad authorised entertainment catalogue, a distinctive viewing and discovery experience, and brand partnerships designed around relevant stories and contexts. The model only becomes durable when content rights, audience trust, campaign delivery and positive unit economics work together.
