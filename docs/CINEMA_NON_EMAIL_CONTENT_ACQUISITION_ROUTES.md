# Cinema — Non-Email Content Acquisition Routes

**Cinema — Experience the World of Stories**

_Status: research and operating plan only. No titles, archive masters, broadcaster feeds, festival projects or distribution rights have been acquired through this document._

## Objective

Build a real catalogue by meeting rights-holders, attending industry markets with an adult representative, working through archives and film schools, and receiving creator submissions. Do not make email response rates the measure of progress.

The launch target remains 40–100 complete, authorised, playable titles. Count only works with documented rights, a valid playback source, required classification/regulatory checks and successful device testing.

## Route 1 — Meet rights-holders at the REFFA 2026 Content Market

- Event: Regal Film Festival and Awards (REFFA) 2026 Content Market
- Published dates: 8–11 December 2026
- Published location: La Palm Royal Beach Hotel, Accra
- Published buyer/seller activities: content-market booths, completed-film showcases and project pitches
- Official event page: https://reffaawards.com/2026-content-market/

**Why this matters:** this is a direct, time-bounded opportunity to meet producers, distributors and completed-film owners, inspect catalogues and learn which titles are actually available for digital licensing.

**Adult-supervised preparation:**
1. Prepare a one-page Cinema buyer profile and a short product demo.
2. Ask for a catalogue and rights-availability sheet; do not ask for files to upload on the spot.
3. Record the exact title, rights-holder, territory, platform rights, licence window, classification status, source format and commercial terms.
4. Ask whether a non-exclusive, low-upfront-cost pilot or revenue-share arrangement is considered. Do not assume one is available.
5. Have an adult representative handle formal negotiations, identity documents, signatures, payments and legal commitments.

Attendance, booth registration or a conversation does not mean a title is licensed.

## Route 2 — Ghana Film Archive and analogue-film digitisation

The Ghana Film Archive describes a project to collect and digitise Ghanaian analogue video works, especially VHS, Super-VHS and Betacam productions from the early 1980s to 1995, and place materials in an accessible repository.

- Project description: https://ghfilmarchive.com/project/
- Archive home: https://ghfilmarchive.com/
- Related project record: https://ifrepo.world/Detail/projects/22
- UCLA Modern Endangered Archives Program project description: https://meap.library.ucla.edu/projects/central-film-library-analogue-video-digitisation-project/

**Potential value for Cinema:** culturally significant Ghanaian films, documentaries and historical audiovisual stories that may be overlooked by contemporary catalogues.

**Important:** digitisation, preservation, public access or archive inclusion does not automatically grant Cinema streaming, commercial exhibition, promotional-clip or sponsorship rights. Identify the actual rights-holder and the archive's authority for each work. The project page lists locations in Kumasi, Accra and Takoradi, but this document does not verify a public walk-in office or direct licensing contact. Arrange a meeting through an official institutional route with an adult; do not arrive unannounced or assume the archive can license every work.

## Route 5 — Schools, film departments and independent creators

Create a single creator submission invitation on Cinema and ask film schools, media departments, campus film clubs and recognised creative organisations to share it.

Priority formats:
- Graduate and student short films with clear written ownership permissions.
- Documentaries and factual programmes.
- Cultural, history, language and community stories.
- Animation and family programming with suitable age classification.
- Independent series, interviews and original creator programmes.
- Licensed live channels and sports documentaries/programmes, not unauthorised match feeds.

For student work, confirm whether the student, school, producer, funder or another party controls the relevant rights. Do not assume the person who uploaded the film owns all music, performance, script, archive-footage and artwork rights.

## What Cinema asks for at a first meeting

1. A current catalogue or slate of completed works.
2. A list of titles available for Ghanaian digital streaming.
3. The legal rights-holder and the person's authority to negotiate.
4. Whether web, mobile and Smart TV VOD rights are available.
5. Whether promotional clips, stills, trailers and sponsorship integrations are permitted.
6. Licence window, exclusivity, holdbacks and other platforms already carrying the work.
7. Approved master delivery method, runtime, resolution, audio tracks, subtitles and captions.
8. Existing classification certificates or other applicable regulatory status.
9. Commercial terms, including any minimum guarantee, upfront fee, revenue share, reporting and payment schedule.
10. Whether a one-to-three-title, non-exclusive pilot is possible.

Do not promise guaranteed viewers, sponsorship, revenue, payment dates or launch placement.

## Rights and technical acceptance gate

A title cannot enter Cinema's published catalogue until an authorised reviewer confirms:

- Rights-holder identity and authority.
- Exact work/edition and all relevant underlying rights.
- Written rights grant covering territory, VOD streaming, web/mobile/Smart TV and the licence period.
- Separate permission for trailers, clips, artwork, sponsorship and brand integrations where needed.
- Ghana classification and applicable platform licensing/regulatory requirements.
- Approved full-length playback master or authorised stream.
- Secure delivery and storage, technical compatibility and successful playback testing.
- Correct title metadata, language, subtitles, credits, advisories and age rating.
- Signed agreement and a recorded approval/review trail.

A trailer, TMDB listing, YouTube link, festival selection, archive listing or public web player does not satisfy this gate.

## Ghana regulatory gate — must be checked before public commercial operation

The National Film Authority's published licence information includes a category for a Film Library / VOD Streaming Platform. Its published classification guidance says audiovisual works intended for public exhibition online are within the classification process.

- Licensing information: https://nfa.gov.gh/apply-for-film-license/
- Classification procedure: https://nfa.gov.gh/film-classification-procedure/
- NFA public contact: +233 50 984 6023; info@nfa.gov.gh

Cinema must obtain current guidance from the NFA on the precise category, applicant eligibility, fees, company requirements, classification responsibilities and whether any separate distributor/exhibitor obligations apply to its intended model. The published pages say the Authority issues an invoice based on the qualifying subcategory; therefore do not assume the platform can launch legally at zero cost. An adult representative and qualified Ghanaian legal/regulatory adviser must review the position before applications, payments, agreements or public exhibition.

## Pipeline discipline

Use these statuses consistently:

- **Lead found:** public source identifies an organisation or work.
- **Contact route verified:** official route or event details confirmed.
- **Meeting requested:** a request has actually been made.
- **Catalogue received:** catalogue received from an authorised source.
- **Rights under review:** exact title and rights evidence being checked.
- **Terms under review:** draft commercial terms being assessed.
- **Licensed:** signed rights agreement is in place.
- **Technical approved:** authorised master passes playback and metadata checks.
- **Published:** all rights, regulatory, technical and internal approvals complete.

Never label a lead, public catalogue or verbal conversation as licensed or acquired. Maintain one record per title, not just one record per supplier.
