## Outreach questions for every library partner

1. Do you own, represent or have authority to sublicense the titles in the catalogue?
2. Can you share a current catalogue spreadsheet with title, format, runtime, year, language, synopsis, artwork and trailer?
3. Which titles are available for third-party VOD distribution in Ghana, Nigeria and the diaspora?
4. Are non-exclusive rights available? What exclusivity, term and territory restrictions apply?
5. Can a pilot start with 5–10 titles and expand if playback and audience response are good?
6. Are there no-upfront, revenue-share or modest minimum-guarantee options? Do not assume any model is accepted.
7. What are the required video masters, subtitles, artwork, metadata and delivery method?
8. Who handles classification, music clearances, performer releases and other underlying rights?
9. Can Cinema retain its own TMDB discovery metadata and use supplied official assets with permission?
10. What reporting, payment, audit, takedown and renewal obligations apply?

## Cinema technical preservation rule

Any authorised titles added later must be additive:
- Keep existing TMDB search, discovery, posters, metadata and trailers.
- Match an authorised programme to an existing TMDB title where possible; do not overwrite TMDB metadata with unverified supplier data.
- Store rights and playback source information separately from TMDB discovery data.
- A trailer or YouTube embed is not a full-film licence or Cinema-hosted master.
- Do not ingest, mirror, scrape, or rehost a platform's stream. Obtain a direct delivery master or expressly authorised playback integration from the rights holder/distributor.
- No production changes should be made until an adult-approved, narrowly scoped implementation is reviewed. No schema, RLS or production-data changes under the current project constraints.
