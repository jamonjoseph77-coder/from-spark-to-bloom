# Cinema Content Partner Starter Pack

**Cinema — Experience the World of Stories**

_Status: internal working document. This is an outreach and diligence pack, not a signed offer, rights agreement, legal opinion, or statement that Cinema has secured content or partners._

## 1. The first objective

Build towards a **40–100 title launch catalogue** of complete, authorised and watchable programmes. The number is a target, not a claim that Cinema already has these titles. Count a title only after its rights are documented, the full programme is playable on Cinema, and its territory/classification checks are complete. Prioritise actual viewing over posters, metadata pages or trailers.

Initial catalogue mix target (adjust to what is actually cleared):
- 15–25 Ghanaian, Nigerian and wider African films/series.
- 10–20 documentaries, factual programmes and cultural stories.
- 10–20 short films and animation.
- 5–15 original creator programmes, interviews and explainers.
- 0–20 open-licensed or public-domain films, only after item-level Ghana rights checks.

Do not fill gaps with fake availability, trailers presented as full films, scraped streams or unlicensed TV/sports feeds.

Do not promise guaranteed views, revenue, audience size, exclusivity or a launch date before Cinema can substantiate it.


## 2. Free and low-friction content routes — reduce cold-email dependence

Cinema should not depend on sending hundreds of cold emails. Build a rights-cleared pipeline from four lanes:

1. **Creator self-submission:** Make the existing Publisher submission route easy to find. Invite filmmakers, schools, film clubs, student creators, churches/community media, documentary makers and production collectives to submit work they own or control. A submission is not automatically approved; retain rights evidence and adult review.
2. **Open-licensed films:** Start with catalogues where the creator has explicitly granted reuse rights. The Studio's OS Video catalogue lists 20 downloadable open films and labels each item with its licence; it includes Blender open films and a CC0 feature. Review each title's exact licence, attribution, share-alike and territory implications before onboarding: https://www.thestudio.io/osvideo/.
3. **Public-domain candidates:** Wikimedia Commons and Internet Archive have film collections, but a website label or uploader declaration is not enough. Copyright is territorial, restorations/soundtracks may have separate rights, and Archive.org says it cannot guarantee uploader-supplied rights claims. Check every title for Ghana (and any other launch territory), exact edition, soundtrack, subtitles and source file before use: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Films_in_the_public_domain and https://archivesupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360014759692-Rights.
4. **Distributor catalogues:** Use one catalogue request to a distributor rather than contacting every filmmaker individually. Okada Media describes itself as a pan-African film sales/distribution company with a catalogue available for licensing: https://www.okadamedia.com/. This is a licensing route, not free content or a confirmed partnership. Other possible catalogues include Alternativa Distribution (https://alternativa.film/distribution) and Organized Khaoz (https://organizedkhaozgh.com/).

## 3. How to reach 40–100 without mass cold-emailing

- Publish one clear creator call on Cinema's website and approved social channels: “Have a film, series, documentary or original programme you own? Submit it to Cinema.”
- Ask schools, universities, film clubs, youth media programmes and community arts groups to share the submission page; they can distribute one link instead of handling individual title enquiries.
- Invite creators to submit a whole slate through one form, not one email per title.
- Offer a simple non-exclusive pilot with no upfront fee promised; explain the actual benefits Cinema can deliver (a watch page, proper credits, audience reporting when available, and promotion only where agreed).
- Use one distributor catalogue request to ask for a list of titles, territories, rights windows, technical formats and minimum commercial terms.
- Create a referral route so a filmmaker can invite another rights holder. Do not pay referral fees unless an adult-reviewed written programme exists.
- Keep a weekly rights-and-playback review queue. Do not publish unverified submissions just to reach the number.

## 4. What Cinema is offering

Cinema is building a digital entertainment home where viewers can discover and watch stories across movies, series, documentaries and other authorised programming.

For an initial content partner, propose a **small, non-exclusive pilot**:
1. Select one to three titles that the partner has authority to license.
2. Agree the permitted countries, platforms, start/end dates and promotional rights in writing.
3. Receive a partner-authorised playback master or delivery link.
4. Complete rights, classification, playback and metadata checks.
5. Publish only after written approval and all required regulatory steps.
6. Share a simple performance report using real, clearly defined metrics.

Commercial terms should be discussed title by title. Cinema should not promise an upfront licence fee or revenue share until the business has agreed funding, delivery costs, reporting rules and payment responsibilities in writing.

## 8. Rights checklist — do not publish until complete

For every title, record and verify:

- Exact title, format, runtime, language and content owner.
- Legal name of the rights holder and the representative's authority to license it.
- Evidence of ownership or distribution authority (not just a verbal assurance).
- Rights granted: on-demand streaming, live transmission, clips, trailers, artwork and promotional use — each separately specified.
- Territory: Ghana, Nigeria, worldwide or named countries.
- Licence start date, end date and any holdbacks or exclusivity.
- Permitted platforms and devices: web, mobile and smart TV.
- Whether downloads, offline viewing, advertising, sponsorship or brand integrations are allowed.
- Technical delivery format, source reliability and replacement procedure.
- Classification/certification status and any age rating or content advisories.
- Signed agreement, reviewer, approval date and renewal/removal process.

A submission form or checkbox is not independent proof of rights. A trailer, public web link or YouTube upload does not prove Cinema has permission to stream the full programme.

## 9. Regulatory checks before launch

**Ghana:** The National Film Authority publishes licensing categories that include VOD streaming platforms/film libraries and states that films intended for public exhibition online require classification. Cinema must obtain current, qualified guidance on which licences and classification steps apply to its exact operating model before making titles available to the public. Official starting points: https://nfa.gov.gh/apply-for-film-license/ and https://nfa.gov.gh/film-classification-procedure/.

**Nigeria:** The NFVCB publishes online exhibition licensing categories for platforms and start-ups. Its current published schedule includes a small platform/start-up category with a stated ₦5,000,000 licence fee and ₦5,000,000 minimum share capital. This may materially affect whether and when Cinema can serve Nigerian viewers. Do not assume a Ghana-based service is exempt; obtain current professional guidance from NFVCB before offering Nigerian film exhibition. Official starting points: https://nfvcb.gov.ng/services/licensing and https://nfvcb.gov.ng/contact.

These are regulatory flags, not legal advice. Requirements and fees can change. A trusted adult and a qualified Ghanaian/Nigerian legal or regulatory adviser must verify the current position before any public launch, agreement or payment.

## 10. First-pilot acceptance test

A title is ready only when all are true:

- [ ] Rights holder and signing authority verified.
- [ ] Written licence executed and stored securely.
- [ ] Territory, window, platforms and promotional permissions recorded.
- [ ] Classification/regulatory requirements checked and satisfied.
- [ ] Full-length playback source supplied by an authorised party.
- [ ] Playback tested on a phone and desktop; failures documented and fixed.
- [ ] Poster, synopsis, language, credits and advisories approved.
- [ ] Sponsor/advertising permissions explicitly agreed or marked not permitted.
- [ ] Launch approval recorded by an authorised Cinema reviewer.

## 11. What Cinema should measure in the pilot

Report only metrics Cinema actually captures and can explain:
- Title page visits.
- Playback starts.
- Successful starts and playback failures.
- Watch time and completion methodology.
- Returning viewers, where privacy rules permit.
- Geography only at an appropriate aggregate level.
- Sponsor delivery evidence, if a sponsor is part of the signed pilot.

Do not sell personal viewer data. Do not present impressions, views or projected sponsor value as money earned. Any revenue share, fee or sponsorship payment must be defined in a signed agreement and recorded only when actually due or received.

## 15. YouTube embeds — useful discovery and viewing, not Cinema's monetisation engine

Official YouTube embeds may be a low-cost way to make selected creator-authorised videos playable while Cinema builds its own licensed catalogue. This is a separate delivery lane from hosting an authorised MP4/HLS master. Do not treat every public YouTube upload as a rights grant: verify the uploader/channel is the rights holder or authorised distributor, confirm embedding is enabled, and check whether the exact film's rights and territory permit the intended use.

**Important commercial limitation:** YouTube's published help page says only YouTube and the video owner earn revenue from ads on embedded videos; the third-party site/app embedding the player does not receive a share. A creator may receive YouTube revenue under their own monetisation arrangements, but Cinema must not promise that YouTube embed views will fund Cinema or create a Cinema-to-creator revenue split. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/132596.

**Player and policy requirements to design around:**
- Use the official YouTube embedded player / IFrame Player API; do not download, rehost, extract, strip ads from, or alter YouTube audiovisual content.
- Keep the player and its normal controls, branding, metadata and ad experience intact. Do not place Cinema UI over player controls or make the experience misleadingly look like a native Cinema-hosted film.
- Do not charge viewers to watch a YouTube-embedded video or add a video-specific gate beyond pressing play. Do not incentivise or reward views, likes, shares or subscriptions.
- Ensure the embed has the required visible player size, correct HTTP Referer context and API identity; a missing Referer can cause playback error 153.
- Check Made for Kids status and apply the required data-collection/privacy handling. If Cinema is designed for children, complete YouTube's required child-directed-app designation before embedding.
- Age-restricted videos may not play on third-party sites. Keep a fallback link to the official YouTube page where permitted.
- Avoid autoplay by default; if later enabled, follow YouTube's visibility and single-autoplay-player requirements.
- Do not sell sponsorships or promotions placed on/within YouTube audiovisual content or the YouTube player without YouTube's prior written approval. A separate Cinema sponsorship around the title needs careful separation from the player and rights-holder permission; do not imply it is allowed without checking policy.

Official technical and policy references:
- YouTube API Services Developer Policies: https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies
- Developer policy compliance guide: https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies-guide
- Embedded player and IFrame API parameters: https://developers.google.com/youtube/player_parameters
- Official embed help and age-restriction / Referer notes: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/171780

**Cinema product decision:** classify these items as `external_platform_embed`, not `cinema_hosted`. Store the canonical YouTube video/channel identifiers, official source URL, rights-holder verification notes, embed-enabled check date, Made for Kids status, age/suitability review, territory notes and last playback test. Keep YouTube playback visually and technically distinct from Cinema-hosted licensed titles. Do not count an embed as a title with Cinema-controlled playback or as a Cinema monetised title.

**Broadcasters and live feeds:** the same principle applies to Al Jazeera, DW, France 24, Africanews and local TV stations. A public live page is evidence that the broadcaster offers a stream to viewers, not proof that Cinema may syndicate, repackage, monetise or embed it in its own app. Obtain the broadcaster's written permission or published syndication licence for Cinema's exact platforms, territories, ad/sponsor treatment and usage window before integration. Until then, link to the official live page rather than re-streaming or presenting it as a Cinema channel.
