## Why this list exists

Cinema needs complete programmes, not only posters, metadata or trailers. Public archive catalogues can help identify older films and educational/documentary works, but an archive's access copy does not automatically grant commercial streaming rights. Public-domain status can vary by country and by the underlying components of a film.

The Library of Congress states that its National Film Registry public-domain film collection contains selections that are public domain. It lists the titles below as part of that collection:
https://www.loc.gov/free-to-use/public-domain-films-from-the-national-film-registry/

## Other useful discovery catalogues

- Internet Archive / Archive Watch catalogue: https://archivewatch.org/
- Wikimedia Commons public-domain film category: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Films_in_the_public_domain
- Creative Commons film index: https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Films
- Library of Congress moving-image copy and rights guidance: https://www.loc.gov/programs/audio-visual-conservation/using-the-collections/copies-of-film-and-video/

The catalogues are discovery tools, not blanket commercial licences. Internet Archive item-level rights statements and uploader assertions require independent checking. Wikimedia category membership also needs item-level source and rights verification.

## Mandatory Ghana and territory review

Before Cinema streams any candidate in Ghana or another country, an adult representative and qualified rights adviser must establish:

1. The copyright status in each intended territory, including Ghana—not only the United States.
2. Whether the exact restored/digitised edition has separate restoration, music, score, subtitle, narration or compilation rights.
3. Whether the source permits commercial VOD streaming, rehosting and delivery on web, mobile and Smart TV.
4. Whether the source copy may be downloaded and stored by Cinema, or only linked/played at the source.
5. Whether the rights holder, archive or donor has imposed access or distribution restrictions.
6. Whether the title requires classification or any other local regulatory approval.
7. Whether artwork, promotional clips, performer likenesses and sponsor associations have separate restrictions.

The Library of Congress explains that many moving-image holdings are copyrighted or subject to donor restrictions, and that it supplies copies only when copyright, preservation and donor restrictions allow. It also notes that determining public-domain status can require individual ownership-record research:
https://www.loc.gov/programs/audio-visual-conservation/using-the-collections/copies-of-film-and-video/

## Acceptance rule

A title stays in **Research Lead** until written evidence supports the proposed commercial use in the target territory and an authorised master passes Cinema's technical checks. Only then may it move through rights review, technical approval and publication. No titles from this research list should be inserted into production as playable Cinema content before those gates pass.

## Immediate work queue

- Select 3–5 candidates from the list for item-level Ghana territory and master research.
- Locate the official catalogue record and exact edition for each candidate.
- Record rights statement, access restrictions, attribution, download/streaming permission and source date.
- Ask an adult representative to review any proposed archive or rights-holder contact.
- Keep Ghanaian and African rights-holder acquisition as the primary content priority; archive titles are supplementary catalogue depth, not a replacement for local stories.
