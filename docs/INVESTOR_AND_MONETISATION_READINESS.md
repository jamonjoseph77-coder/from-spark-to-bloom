# Cinema — Investor And Monetisation Readiness

_Last reviewed: 6 November 2026_

## Positioning

Cinema is a next-generation television service and digital entertainment home. It brings premium video, live programming, discovery, sharing and title-centred conversations into one connected experience across mobile, web and television.

Cinema is not positioned as another subscription streaming service or a social-media feed. Its product principles are accessibility, content delivery, everyday television use and opening up the world of storytelling.

## Product currently represented in the codebase

- Home discovery, movies, series, news, sports, live-TV destinations and category-specific experiences.
- Published Cinema title records, title pages, trailers and approved linked short-form video.
- Dedicated vertical Shorts experience.
- My Cinema and viewing progress features.
- Friends, Circle, profiles and title-linked conversations.
- Publisher/studio identity and verification workflow.
- Admin tools for publishing titles and clips.

A passing build or a deployed website does not, by itself, prove that every catalogue item has playback rights, that every stream is reliable, or that Cinema has paying users. Demonstrations should use content that Cinema is authorised to show.

## Monetisation direction

Cinema's current commercial direction is a title-centred Digital Box Office, not viewer subscriptions and not intrusive mid-roll advertising.

Potential revenue lines, each subject to signed rights and partner approval:

1. **Title and programme sponsorship** — a brand underwrites an eligible title, episode, programme or live event.
2. **Contextual title placements** — clearly labelled, relevant partner placements around a title page or an intentional pause, never disguised as editorial content.
3. **Premiere and watch-party partnerships** — sponsors support an approved premiere, event or viewing experience.
4. **Licensed premium windows** — a rights-holder authorises a time-limited paid or sponsored access window where the contract permits it.
5. **B2B distribution and reporting** — rights-holders receive agreed delivery and campaign reporting, using privacy-respecting aggregated metrics.

Cinema should account for each content property separately: gross partner receipts, taxes and payment costs, direct delivery costs, rights-holder allocation, partner fulfilment costs and Cinema's retained revenue. The commercial terms must be agreed before a campaign or paid window is activated.

## What is not yet established

Do not present any of the following as already live unless independently verified:

- Signed studio, broadcaster, creator or brand agreements.
- Licensed rights to distribute particular films, episodes, sports or live channels.
- A production payment checkout, escrow, settlement or automated rights-holder payout system.
- Revenue, paying customers, viewership, retention or audience growth figures.
- Guaranteed playback for third-party embeds or streams.

The current Digital Box Office page describes a commercial framework; it is not a viewer purchase checkout. No payment processor or financial settlement flow should be enabled until the company has approved commercial terms, rights, compliance and operational controls.

## Minimum commercial launch checklist

- Obtain written distribution, promotion, clip and territory rights for every commercial title and stream.
- Sign a separate agreement for each sponsor campaign, including deliverables, placement, dates, approvals, reporting and cancellation terms.
- Define revenue attribution and the rights-holder / partner / Cinema allocation before collecting funds.
- Select an appropriate payment and invoicing provider after legal and finance review; test refunds, failed payments, reconciliation and settlement.
- Establish business banking, bookkeeping, tax treatment, privacy notices, data retention and customer support processes.
- Keep sponsor material clearly labelled and separate from editorial recommendations.
- Test the complete user journey on low-bandwidth mobile networks and common devices.
- Maintain a content-rights register and an incident / takedown process.

Formal contracts, company obligations, payments and investor discussions should be reviewed with a trusted adult and qualified legal/financial advisers before anyone signs or commits funds.

## Investor evidence to assemble

Use verified evidence only:

- Live product URL and a short guided demo.
- Screenshots or a short screen recording of the actual working journeys.
- Current product scope and what remains incomplete.
- User interviews, pilot results and usage analytics once collected.
- Rights-holder and sponsor pipeline, clearly labelled as prospects until signed.
- A 12–18 month use-of-funds plan with assumptions, milestones and runway.
- A realistic Ghana-first launch plan, including bandwidth, content rights and distribution costs.

Never invent user counts, revenue, partnerships, licensing rights or investor commitments. Label forecasts as forecasts and assumptions as assumptions.

## Suggested first commercial pilot

Start with one rights-cleared title or programme and one sponsor. Agree the placement, campaign period, deliverables, reporting and revenue split in writing. Run a small pilot, reconcile the costs and receipts, report the outcome to both parties, then decide whether to repeat. Do not build a complex payment marketplace before this first transaction is proven.
